Scoring system

Public federal data at census-tract resolution

What goes into the score
  • HailNRI hail risk: how often damaging hail falls times the building value exposed, scored 0 to 100 nationally. Half of Texas housing scores above 88.
  • Wind and tornadoNRI strong wind and tornado risk: the same build for straight-line wind and tornadoes. Together with hail these are the roof and siding claims.
  • FloodNRI inland flood risk: river and flash flooding times the value exposed. The tier rules also use the share of a ZIP's residents in tracts FEMA rates Relatively High or Very High for inland flooding, which picks out the creek and floodplain neighborhoods.
  • AvailabilityCarrier models (roof age, roof material, elevation) are property-level and not published by ZIP. This composite uses public federal data on the same hazards. Hurricane and coastal flooding are in the spreadsheet but not scored, since none of your leads so far came from the coast.

Tier rules
  • ExtremeComposite score 84+, or a ZIP hail score of 98+
  • HighComposite score 77+, or hail score 96+, or 75%+ of the ZIP's residents in tracts rated Relatively High or Very High for inland flooding
  • ModerateComposite score 71+, or hail score 92+, or 40%+ of residents in high-flood tracts
  • LowEverything else. Because nearly all of Texas is above the national median for hail, the cut points are set within Texas, so Low still means a real hail exposure.
  • How Extreme is setExtreme marks about one home in 9 (11.4% of housing): the top of the Texas hail and storm range.

Home campaign: recommended treatment

Extreme tier gets a roof and wind and hail deductible review · High tier gets a roof and deductible check

Home treatment by tier
  • ExtremeRoof and wind and hail deductible review. Ask roof age and material on every quote, explain the wind and hail deductible in dollars, and mention impact-resistant roofing, which many Texas policies credit.
  • HighRoof and deductible check. Ask roof age and walk through the wind and hail deductible. In the flood-driven High ZIPs, offer a separate flood policy too: a standard homeowners policy does not cover flood.
  • ModerateStandard quote. Roof age is still worth asking; hail reaches most of the state.
  • LowStandard quote.
These tiers describe where a conversation matters most, not where to stop advertising. Many of your best North Texas ZIPs are High or Extreme for hail, and those homeowners are the ones who most need a well-explained policy. We do not recommend excluding any ZIP on weather risk. Hail and storm in the conversation

Questions that make a Texas home quote faster and stickier

Why it matters here
  • Roof ageHail is the defining Texas Home hazard, and roof age and material shape both the price and the claim. Asking at the first conversation avoids a re-quote later.
  • DeductiblesMany Texas policies carry a separate wind and hail deductible, often a percentage of the dwelling limit. Explaining it in dollars at quote time avoids a surprise after the first storm claim.
  • Impact-resistant roofsClass 4 impact-resistant shingles earn a premium credit with many Texas carriers. In High and Extreme ZIPs, asking about the roof type can lower the quote.
  • Tornado and wind63% of Dallas-Fort Worth housing sits in ZIPs scoring 80+ nationally for tornado. Roofs and fences are the claim; the conversation is the same.
  • FloodFlash flooding is the other Texas Home hazard and is not covered by a standard homeowners policy. In flood-driven ZIPs, mostly around San Antonio and Houston, a separate flood policy is worth offering.

Data & files

  • Risk sourceFEMA National Risk Index, census tract table, December 2025 release (hail, strong wind, tornado, inland flooding, hurricane, coastal flooding), all 6,883 Texas tracts.
  • GeographyU.S. Census Bureau 2020 ZIP Code Tabulation Areas and the 2020 ZCTA-to-tract relationship file. The file reports land-area overlaps, not population, so each tract's values are weighted into a ZIP by the tract's population times the share of its land inside the ZIP. Housing counts from ACS 2020-2024. Map outlines from Census TIGERweb.
  • LeadsAll 127 GOAL Home lead records for this account, October 2 to October 29, 2026, counted by the ZIP on each lead; all 127 matched a populated Texas ZIP. Only counts by ZIP are used.
  • LimitsA ZIP score describes the ZIP, not a house. The roof, its age and the home's elevation decide each quote; the tiers decide where to ask first.
  • FilesJH-TX-ZIP-Data-10-30-26.csv · every ZIP, every score, tier and conversation column, hurricane and coastal flood for reference, plus the demographics used in the companion report and your Home lead and sold counts by ZIP.
